First Section: The Ruling Regarding Words Against Divine Majesty

We now consider the ruling concerning a person who attributes to Allah عز وجل such matters as are unbefitting His majesty, whether by way of insult or due to interpretation, ijtihad, personal desire, or innovation, such as giving Him resemblance, describing Him with a body part, or denying a quality of His, and so on.

This is a matter regarding which the scholars of the Salaf and later generations have differed concerning whether such a speaker and believer is to be declared a kafir. Disagreement is also transmitted from Imam Malik رحمة الله تعالى عليه and his students in this. However, when such people form a group and gain strength, there is no disagreement that war and jihad against them is to be waged. Repentance will be sought from them; if they repent, well and good; otherwise they are to be killed.

The disagreement among the scholars concerns only an individual. Imam Malik رحمة الله تعالى عليه and his students refrain from declaring him a kafir and from killing him; rather, his punishment is to be prolonged so that he may return and make his repentance public.

Isa رحمة الله تعالى عليه asked Ibn al-Qasim رحمة الله تعالى عليه concerning the people of desire, meaning the Ibadis, Qadaris, and similar innovators who oppose Ahl al-Sunnat wa al-Jama'at and distort and interpret the divine Book. He said that repentance should be sought from them, whether they manifest their belief or conceal it. If they repent, well and good; otherwise they are to be killed, and their inheritance goes to their heirs. Similarly, Ibn al-Qasim رحمة الله تعالى عليه said in the Book of Muhammad concerning the Qadari sect and the like.

Ibn al-Qasim رحمة الله تعالى عليه said that if a person says that Allah عز وجل did not speak to Hazrat Musa عليه السلام, repentance is to be sought from him. If he repents, well and good; otherwise he is to be killed. Ibn Habib رحمة الله تعالى عليه and others consider him a kafir. It is also narrated from Sahnoon رحمة الله تعالى عليه concerning a person who said that Allah عز وجل has no speech: he said, "He is a kafir."

Imam Malik رحمة الله تعالى عليه said that whoever believes in a body for the essence of Allah, such as a hand, ear, or eye, then that same limb of the speaker should be cut from his body, because he likened Allah to himself. He also gave the fatwa of kufr regarding a person who said that the Qur'an is created, and ordered that he be killed. In the narration of Ibn Nafi' رحمة الله تعالى عليه, he is to be flogged, given a painful beating, and imprisoned until he repents. In one narration, Imam Malik's رحمة الله تعالى عليه position is that he is to be killed and his repentance is not accepted.
